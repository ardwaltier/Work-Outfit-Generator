How recommendations work
Weather wins: choose Today or Tomorrow; the selected day’s temperature, feels-like temperature, forecast high and precipitation are the strongest inputs. The cream Hugo Boss quarter-zip is your lightest sweater. The washed-deep-navy Quince fleece quarter-zip is warmer and more casual, with a hard forecast-high ceiling around 62°F. The cream Hugo Boss quarter-zip and can appear into the mid-60s; heavier sweaters are held for cooler weather, and the L.L.Bean Waterfowl sweater is reserved mostly for sub-50°F days. Outerwear is optional rather than part of every office outfit. The charcoal Canada Goose quilted liner is a high-priority winter workday layer for cold, mostly dry conditions or light rain when outdoor exposure is brief. The navy Barbour Ashby remains the preferred cool/wet fall-winter jacket, while the dark-brown UNIQLO blouson is the year-round mild-rain backup. The olive Loewe quilted jacket is a style-forward cool-weather option for mostly dry days. Warm, dry summer days generally get no jacket suggestion. Dark-brown cords are primarily October–March and are excluded in warmer weather. Forecast high is the hard ceiling for warm/heavy tops: the denim shirt to about 77°F, rugby shirts can go up to 72°F, soft-woven shirts to about 68°F, the cream waffle thermal to 62°F, the brown Filson thermal to 60°F, and the heavyweight charcoal flannel to about 58°F. Effective temperature still helps rank eligible outfits, but it cannot override those ceilings. Your 28″ ABC pants are polo-first and Jordan-only—boots and Stan Smiths are excluded with them. Boots are completely excluded from May through September, and from October through April they are only considered when the effective temperature is below 65°F. The picker favors contrast (dark pants with lighter tops, khaki with darker tops), avoids overly brown combinations without separation, treats black dress Blundstones as the more polished boot and Tobacco #1911s as the more casual/rugged boot, and keeps jeans in casual-work territory. The calendar month then nudges color, texture and footwear without overriding unusual weather. Each generation returns three choices: Practical / Comfortable, Balanced, and Style-forward. Confirmed weekday outfits are saved locally and the five most recent appear in Recent Workwear above this section. You can also use “Add past outfit” to backfill a missed Monday–Friday entry, and the rotation history will update from that saved date. View All Workwear keeps the complete confirmed history and can filter the last 30, 60, 90 days or all time. Export CSV creates an Excel-friendly file of the full history. Year in Review summarizes the current year with most-worn outfits plus top and bottom clothing rankings. You can edit or delete those entries; changing a past top rebuilds the rotation history automatically. “I liked this outfit” can be used either on a current recommendation or from Recent Workwear. Once an exact outfit has been liked, its button shows “Liked ✓” so you can see that the preference is already saved. Likes add only a small capped preference bonus to that outfit and similar style traits, so weather and hard rules still dominate. If you modify a recommendation, open “Change what I’m wearing” and select the pants, top, shoes, and optional layer you actually chose. Then tap “I’m wearing this”; the app logs the actual selected top for rotation, and the selected Today/Tomorrow date is used as the wear date; that top is then held out until it is in a later work week and at least 4 business days have passed between wears (for example, Friday → next Thursday). All three obey the same hard weather and wardrobe rules, but each is scored differently so you can choose between ease, the best all-around option, or a more fashion-driven look. The off-white On THE ROGER Clubhouse Pro is treated as the middle-ground neutral sneaker: more polished than the Jordans and sportier than the Stan Smiths. The five UNIQLO soft-woven cotton shirts are treated as flannel-adjacent transitional pieces for fall, winter and cooler early-spring days. The charcoal Amazon Essentials shirt is treated as a true heavyweight flannel for colder conditions. The Abercrombie brown/charcoal plaid overshirt is treated as a removable chilly-day layer, with black jeans strongly preferred and the black/khaki T-shirts or cream/Filson thermals used as its base layers. The Loewe olive quilted jacket is treated as the sole green element in an outfit and is never paired with green pants or green tops. Wardrobe names shown in the app use your preferred naming convention while the recommendation rules keep stable internal identifiers behind the scenes. Every wardrobe item is included in the outfit engine; curated combinations are preferred, and compatible fallback combinations are generated automatically so Build Around an Item works for every piece.
